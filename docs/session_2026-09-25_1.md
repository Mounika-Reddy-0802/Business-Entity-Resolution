## What the real data taught us

- Names repeat across many businesses (86 US S1 "Blue Hypnosis"): blocking keys must combine
  name and location; name-only keys over-block and get skipped.
- The truth is strictly one-to-one (no S2/S3 id in two S1 lists): one-to-one assignment and
  competition features are safe and strong.
- ~23% of Indian S2/S3 names are in native scripts; a consonant skeleton after transliteration
  brings them close to the Latin spelling, and an address-pair key catches the rest.
- On 16 GB, everything must be chunked: Arrow strings, per-kind hash joins spilled to disk,
  per-chunk scoring, per-country features, batched string similarity.
